# Rental Yield & ROI Mechanics

Gross rental yield is the simplest calculation and the one most often quoted by brokers. You take the annual rent the property generates, divide it by the purchase price, and multiply by 100. A unit bought for AED 1,200,000 that rents for AED 96,000 per year yields 8%. That number is useful for a quick comparison across listings, but it is not the number that hits your bank account.

Net rental yield strips out the carrying costs. In Dubai, the most common deductions are:

- **Dubai Land Department (DLD) registration fee**: 4% of the purchase price, paid once at acquisition. Some investors amortize this over a 5-year hold period and factor it into annual cost.
- **Service charges**: Annual fees paid to the building or community management, covering maintenance, security, gyms, pools, and common area utilities. These vary wildly. A villa in Dubai Hills Estate might run AED 15, 25 per square foot per year. An apartment in an older part of Bur Dubai could be half that. Always ask for the service charge statement from the developer or management company before closing.
- **Property management fee**: If you use a firm to handle tenant sourcing, maintenance calls, and rent collection, expect 5, 8% of the annual rent. Some charge a flat monthly fee, typically AED 500, 1,500 depending on the unit type and location.
- **Vacancy allowance**: No property rents 365 days a year. A realistic assumption for Dubai is 4, 6 weeks vacancy between tenancies. That is roughly 8, 12% of the annual rent you should subtract before calculating yield. In oversupplied areas like Dubai Marina or JLT, vacancy can stretch to 8, 10 weeks. In high-demand villa communities with limited supply, it may be 2, 3 weeks.
- **Ejari registration and renewal fees**: Minor, around AED 200, 500 annually, but they add up.
- **DEWA connection and security deposits**: One-time or refundable, but still cash out of pocket.

A net yield calculation for that same AED 1,200,000 unit might look like this: AED 96,000 gross rent minus AED 7,680 (8% management fee) minus AED 9,600 (10% vacancy reserve) minus AED 14,000 (service charge at AED 12/sqft for a 1,100 sqft apartment) equals AED 64,720. Net yield: 5.4%. That is the number that matters for cash flow analysis.

Total ROI includes capital appreciation. Dubai has seen periods of 15, 20% annual price growth in certain micro-markets (2013, 2014, 2021, 2022 post-Expo), followed by flat or correcting years. A realistic long-term assumption for mature, well-located communities is 3, 5% annual appreciation, though specific areas like Palm Jumeirah or Emirates Hills have performed differently. To calculate total ROI over a 5-year hold: sum the net rental income across 5 years, add the projected sale price (purchase price plus appreciation), subtract the original purchase price plus all transaction costs (DLD fee, agent commission, legal fees), and divide by the total cash invested. If you put 50% down and financed the rest, borrowing amplifies the return on equity but also the risk.

One honest caveat: rental yield data in Dubai is noisy. Many listing portals and brokers quote gross yields based on asking rents and purchase prices that never transact. A property listed at AED 1,000,000 with a claimed rent of AED 100,000 (10% gross yield) might actually sell for AED 1,100,000 and rent for AED 88,000 (8% gross). Always verify with recent transaction data from the DLD's RERA (Real Estate Regulatory Authority) indices or a broker who can show you actual closed deals, not listings.
