# Self-Audit Checklist for Global Investors

## Developer Track Record

Start with the developer, not the unit. A beautiful apartment in a bad developer's project is a liability. You need to verify three layers: delivery history, financial health, and legal standing.

Look at the developer's past projects. Were they delivered on time? RERA (Real Estate Regulatory Authority) in Dubai publishes a list of developers who have completed projects versus those with delays. Cross-reference the developer name against RERA's "Off-Plan Project Registration" database. If a developer has multiple projects marked as "delayed" or "cancelled," that is a hard stop.

Check for escrow account compliance. Every off-plan project in Dubai must have a registered escrow account with a specific bank. The developer cannot legally withdraw money without RERA-approved milestone completions. Ask for the escrow account number and the bank name. Then verify directly with the bank or through the Dubai Land Department's (DLD) online services. If the developer hesitates to provide this, walk away.

Review the developer's financial statements if available. Publicly listed developers like Emaar, Damac, or Sobha publish annual reports. Private developers are harder to vet. In those cases, look at their track record of completed projects and any news about disputes or litigation. A developer with a decade of completed projects and no major legal battles is a safer bet than a new entrant with flashy marketing.

## Title Deed Verification

Never rely on a seller's word or a broker's PDF. Title deed verification must be done through the DLD's official system. The DLD provides a "Property Inquiry" service on its website and mobile app (Dubai REST). You need the property's "Plot Number" or "Unit Number" and the title deed number.

The inquiry will show:
- Current owner's name (must match the seller's Emirates ID or passport)
- Any mortgages or liens registered against the property
- Encumbrances or court orders
- Property type and usage (residential, commercial, mixed-use)

If there is an existing mortgage, the seller must provide a No Objection Certificate (NOC) from the bank. The NOC confirms the bank allows the sale and states the outstanding loan amount. Without this, the transfer cannot proceed. A common tactic is for sellers to hide an existing mortgage. The DLD check exposes this immediately.

For off-plan properties, you verify the "Off-Plan Sales Agreement" (Oqood) registration. The Oqood is the initial registration of your purchase with DLD. It proves the developer has recorded your unit and your payments. You can check Oqood status through the DLD portal using your contract number.

## Escrow Account Confirmation

Dubai law mandates escrow accounts for off-plan sales. The developer opens an account with a DLD-approved bank. The bank releases funds only after RERA-certified completion stages (e.g., foundation, structure completion, handover).

You need to confirm three things:
1. The escrow account is registered with RERA. RERA publishes a list of approved escrow accounts. Search for the developer's name and project.
2. The bank is a DLD-approved escrow bank (list available on DLD website). Not all banks qualify.
3. The developer's payment plan aligns with the escrow release schedule. If the developer asks for 50% upfront before any construction milestone, that is a red flag. The escrow system is designed to prevent exactly that.

For ready properties, escrow is not applicable. Instead, you use the "MOU" (Memorandum of Understanding) and the "Form F" (transfer form). The MOU is a legally binding contract between buyer and seller. It must include the agreed price, payment schedule, handover date, and any penalties for delay. The Form F is the DLD transfer form that both parties sign at the time of ownership transfer. Always use a registered trustee or a DLD-approved broker to handle the transfer process.

## Exit Strategy Planning

Most investors focus on entry price. The professionals focus on exit. You need a clear plan for when and how you will sell or refinance.

Consider the holding period. Dubai's market cycles historically run 5-7 years. Buying at the peak of a cycle and needing to sell within 2 years is a recipe for loss. Look at historical price data for the specific area (e.g., Dubai Marina, Downtown, Dubai Hills Estate). If prices have risen sharply in the last 18 months, you are buying near the top.

Factor in the "exit costs." When you sell a property in Dubai, you pay:
- DLD transfer fee: 4% of sale price (plus administrative fees, typically AED 500-4,000)
- Broker commission: 2% (negotiable)
- NOC fee from developer: AED 500-5,000
- Mortgage early settlement penalty (if applicable): usually 1-3% of outstanding loan

A typical exit cost is 6-8% of the sale price. If your property appreciates 10% over 5 years, your net gain after costs is closer to 2-4%. That is a thin margin. The math works better with longer holds or when using borrowed money.

For off-plan properties, the exit is often before handover. You can sell your "right to purchase" to another buyer. This is called "flipping" or "assignment." Check the developer's policy on assignments. Some developers allow it with a fee (AED 10,000-50,000). Others prohibit it entirely. If you buy off-plan, know the assignment rules before you commit.

## Tax and Tax Treaty Considerations

Dubai has no personal income tax, no capital gains tax, and no property tax. That is the headline. The nuance is in your home country's tax treatment.

If you are a resident of India, the UK, Canada, or the US, your global income may be taxable. Dubai property rental income and capital gains are likely taxable in your home country. The key question: does your country have a double taxation agreement (DTA) with the UAE?

The UAE has DTAs with over 100 countries. These treaties generally prevent the same income from being taxed twice. For example, under the India-UAE DTA, rental income from UAE property is taxable only in the UAE (where the tax rate is 0%). But the Indian tax authorities require you to declare this income and claim the exemption. If you fail to file, you risk penalties.

For US citizens, the Foreign Account Tax Compliance Act (FATCA) applies. You must report foreign financial accounts including UAE bank accounts and property held through a company. The US-UAE DTA does not cover all situations. Consult a US tax accountant who specializes in expat taxation.

For UK residents, the UK taxes worldwide income. But the UK-UAE DTA allows you to claim foreign tax credit relief. You will still need to file a UK self-assessment return and report the rental income or capital gains.

The common mistake: assuming zero tax in Dubai means zero tax everywhere. It does not. Hire a cross-border tax advisor before you buy, not after you sell. The cost of the advisor (typically AED 5,000-15,000 for a full review) is small compared to a surprise tax bill.

For investors using a corporate structure (e.g., a Dubai Free Zone company or a BVI company), the tax treatment changes further. Some countries have "controlled foreign corporation" (CFC) rules that attribute the company's income to the shareholder. Again, this is not DIY territory.
