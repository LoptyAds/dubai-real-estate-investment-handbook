# Frequently Asked Questions on Dubai Property Investment

## Minimum Investment Amounts

There is no single minimum that applies across all Dubai property. The Dubai Land Department (DLD) sets no statutory floor, but developer requirements and market realities do. For off-plan units, entry-level studios in affordable areas like Jumeirah Village Circle or Dubai South start around AED 500,000 (roughly $136,000). Ready properties in prime locations such as Downtown or Dubai Marina rarely dip below AED 1 million ($272,000). The practical minimum for a non-resident seeking a decent rental yield and capital appreciation is AED 800,000 to AED 1 million. Below that, you are competing in a thin sub-market where exit liquidity is poor and tenant demand is inconsistent.

## Mortgage Options for Non-Residents

Non-residents can get mortgages in Dubai, but the terms differ from what a resident would receive. Most banks lend up to 50% of the property value for expat non-residents. A few lenders stretch to 60% on select ready properties. Interest rates sit roughly 1% to 2% higher than resident rates, currently in the 5.5% to 6.5% range depending on the bank and your profile. You need a minimum property value of AED 1 million to qualify with most mainstream lenders. Documentation required includes six months of bank statements, proof of income, and a credit report from your home country. Some banks accept salary certificates instead. Processing takes 4 to 6 weeks. Pre-approval is advisable before making an offer.

## Visa Pathways

Property ownership does not automatically grant residency. The key visa routes are:

- **Investor Visa**: Requires a property valued at AED 750,000 or more. Valid for 2 years, renewable. You must hold full ownership, not a mortgage exceeding 50% LTV. The visa covers the investor and immediate family.
- **Golden Visa**: For properties worth AED 2 million or more. Valid for 10 years. No sponsorship required. You can be absent from the UAE for any period without losing the visa. The property can be off-plan (with a 50% payment milestone) or ready. Joint ownership with a spouse also qualifies if each share exceeds AED 2 million.

Both routes require a valid passport, title deed, and medical insurance. Processing time is typically 2 to 4 weeks through the General Directorate of Residency and Foreigners Affairs (GDRFA). The Golden Visa is the stronger option if you meet the threshold, because it removes renewal hassle and grants more flexibility.

## Property Transfer Fees

The DLD charges a transfer fee of 4% of the purchase price, payable by the buyer. This is not negotiable. On a AED 1 million property, that is AED 40,000. Additional costs include:

- **Registration fee**: AED 4,000 for properties under AED 500,000, AED 4,000 plus 0.25% of the value above AED 500,000 for higher values.
- **Agent commission**: Typically 2% of the purchase price (plus 5% VAT), though this is negotiable.
- **Mortgage registration fee**: 0.25% of the loan amount plus AED 290.
- **Valuation fee**: AED 2,500 to AED 3,500 if a bank valuation is required.
- **Conveyance fee**: AED 2,000 to AED 3,000 for the administrative transfer.

Total closing costs for a ready property generally run 7% to 8% of the purchase price. Off-plan properties have lower upfront transfer fees (often 1% to 2% plus DLD fees), but the developer may charge administrative fees.

## Repatriation of Funds

The UAE has no currency controls. You can transfer sale proceeds or rental income out of the country freely, provided the funds originated from a legal source and you have the relevant documentation (title deed, sale agreement, bank statements). Banks may ask for proof of the source of funds for amounts above AED 100,000. Transfers are done via SWIFT or online banking. Expect a processing time of 1 to 3 business days. The main practical limitation is that banks may hold funds for 24 to 48 hours for compliance checks. No withholding tax applies on capital gains or rental income for individuals. The UAE does not have a capital gains tax or personal income tax. Repatriation is straightforward, but you should keep a paper trail of every transaction for your home country's tax authority.

## Common Misconception: "I Can Get a Mortgage Before Finding a Property"

No. Banks in Dubai issue mortgages against a specific property, not a pre-approved amount you can shop with. You can get a pre-qualification letter indicating your borrowing capacity, but the final approval and disbursement happen only after the property is identified, valued, and legally vetted. This is a critical difference from markets like the UK or Australia. Plan your timeline accordingly.
